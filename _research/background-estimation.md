A tt̄γ candidate does not always contain a prompt photon and prompt lepton from the intended hard-scatter process. Detector signatures can be mimicked by other particles, and those backgrounds must be estimated before a cross-section can be extracted.

## Electrons reconstructed as photons

An electron can appear photon-like when its charged-particle track is not correctly associated with its electromagnetic calorimeter cluster. I studied this contribution in tt̄γ selections and helped estimate it with data-driven techniques. Control samples enriched in well-understood electron processes provide the probability for an electron signature to migrate into the photon category.

## Non-prompt leptons

Leptons produced in heavy-flavor decays, photon conversions, or misidentified hadrons can pass the analysis selection. Their rate and kinematic shape are difficult to model reliably with simulation alone. I worked on an estimate based on looser and tighter lepton definitions, with efficiencies constrained in data.

Both studies required careful validation of control-region purity, the transfer into the signal region, and systematic uncertainties associated with differences in event composition. These estimates became inputs to the later differential tt̄γ measurement.
